Traditional Street Games

Chile's plazas and backyards have long been stages for simple, joyful games passed down through generations of children.

Rayuela: A National Pastime

Rayuela, Chile's official national sport, involves tossing metal discs called tejos toward a target line filled with clay. Players score points for precision, and the game is a staple at family gatherings, national holidays, and neighborhood competitions across the country.

Trompo and Volantín

The trompo, a wooden spinning top launched with a string, tests balance and timing. Meanwhile, volantín kite flying fills September skies during Independence celebrations, with colorful paper kites and friendly "cutting" duels between competing flyers nationwide.

Everyday Backyard Fun

Simple games keep children active outdoors:

  • Emboque — a cup-and-ball balancing toy
  • La pinta — a chasing and tagging game
  • Luche — a hopscotch variant using chalk grids
